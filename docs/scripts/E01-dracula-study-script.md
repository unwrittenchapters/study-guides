## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1897 · Transylvania, Whitby and London, 1890s]

[upbeat, storytelling]

*Dracula* is told in diaries, letters, telegrams and newspaper cuttings. It begins with a young English solicitor's clerk, Jonathan Harker, travelling to Transylvania to help Count Dracula buy a house in England, an old place called Carfax. At the castle, Jonathan slowly realises he's a prisoner.

The Count sails to England on a Russian ship called the *Demeter*. It crashes onto the beach at Whitby in a storm, with its captain dead and tied to the wheel. A huge dog leaps ashore, and the cargo is fifty boxes of earth. Jonathan's fiancée, Mina Murray, is staying in Whitby with her friend Lucy Westenra, who begins to walk in her sleep and fall ill.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: THE STORM AT WHITBY]

Chapter one. The Storm at Whitby.

Mina Murray is spending the summer in Whitby with her best friend, Lucy Westenra. Every afternoon they sit on a bench in the churchyard on the East Cliff, below the ruined abbey, and Mina writes in her journal. She's worried, because her fiancé, Jonathan, has stopped writing from Transylvania.

On the night of the eighth of August, a great storm blows in. A Russian ship, the *Demeter*, runs aground below the cliff with her captain dead at the wheel. A huge dog leaps ashore and vanishes up the cliff.

[Chapter card flips in: THE CAPTAIN'S LOG]

Chapter two. The Captain's Log.

The newspaper prints the captain's log. In the book, Mina finds it sad and turns the page. Tonight, she reads it twice and makes a list. The cargo was fifty boxes of earth, sent to a solicitor on the Crescent, a few doors from where she's staying.

She puts on her hat and gloves, visits him, and learns the boxes are going by train to Carfax, near London. It's the house Jonathan went abroad to sell, right next to Dr Seward's hospital. Mina writes to Dr Seward, and that night she ties Lucy's door key to her own wrist.

[Chapter card flips in: FIFTY BOXES]

Chapter three. Fifty Boxes.

Dr Seward sends for his old teacher, Professor Van Helsing, who comes at once. He reads Mina's notes and hangs garlic flowers around Lucy's window, and Lucy sleeps safely.

Then the friends go to Carfax and find all fifty boxes together in the old chapel. Van Helsing crumbles a little holy bread into each one. In the book, that's how the earth is made useless to the Count. Mina checks every box against her list, and when they reach the last one, she draws a neat line under the column. Fifty. That's all of them.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

The Count arrives to find nowhere to rest, and quietly leaves England on the ship the *Czarina Catherine*. Lucy marries Arthur, and Mina goes to Budapest to marry Jonathan, just as she does in the book. The next summer, they sit together on the bench above Whitby, and Mina shows him her list.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**Careful noticing.** Mina wins by reading closely, making lists and checking facts. Her careful work shows that patience can be a kind of courage.

**Stories inside stories.** The book is a pile of diaries, letters and news cuttings. Each writer knows only part of the truth until the pieces are put together.

**Old and new.** The Count is ancient, but his hunters use trains, telegrams, typewriters and shorthand. Modern tools help them win.

**Teamwork.** No one in the book wins alone. The friends succeed by sharing what they know.

[Beat. Music sting.]

[curious]

So what could have happened?

[Path card: A · No one listens]

Path A. She shares her worry, but no one listens, and the Count settles into Carfax unnoticed. It could happen: Lucy is ill, so the men are distracted. But Mina never lets a clue go.

[Path card: B · She goes alone]

Path B. She goes to Carfax on her own and walks straight into danger. It could happen: Mina is brave. But she's careful, not reckless.

[Path card: C · She tracks the boxes]

Path C. She makes a careful list, traces all fifty boxes, and brings the facts to Dr Seward and Van Helsing. This one fits the characters: Mina already types everyone's diaries into one record. Van Helsing trusts facts set out in order.

## The real ending

[clearly, storytelling]

So what actually happens in the book?

In the book, the men shut Mina out of the hunt to protect her, and the Count reaches her anyway. Then she becomes the key to tracking him, and they catch him at his castle just before sunset.

[thoughtfully]

Why did Bram Stoker choose it? Stoker shows that keeping Mina out was the mistake. The team only wins when they share everything, including her mind.

[Beat.]

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

Bram Stoker was born in Dublin in 1847. He was ill for much of his childhood and spent years in bed listening to stories. As an adult he managed the Lyceum Theatre in London for nearly thirty years, working for the famous actor Henry Irving. He wrote *Dracula* in his spare time, after years of reading about folklore and visiting Whitby.

## The world of the book

[curious]

*Dracula* comes from the end of Queen Victoria's reign. London had gas lamps, steam trains and telegraphs, and newspapers came out several times a day. Typewriters and shorthand were new office skills, and many young women like Mina were learning them to find work.
