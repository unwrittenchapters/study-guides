## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1817 · Somerset, Lyme and Bath, 1814 to 1815]

[upbeat, storytelling]

Sir Walter Elliot is a vain baronet who has spent too much money. To save it, he rents out his home, Kellynch Hall in Somerset, and moves to Bath. His middle daughter, Anne, is twenty-seven. She's kind and sensible, but her family barely notices her.

Eight years before, Anne was engaged to a young naval officer, Frederick Wentworth. Her friend Lady Russell persuaded her to break it off, because he had no money. Now he's back, a rich captain, and his sister and her husband, Admiral and Mrs Croft, are renting Kellynch.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: KELLYNCH]

Chapter one. Kellynch.

In the summer of 1806, a young naval officer came to stay with his brother in a quiet Somerset village.

His name was Frederick Wentworth. He was clever, bold and full of confidence. Nearby, at Kellynch Hall, lived Anne Elliot. She was nineteen, gentle and kind, and her vain father hardly noticed her. Within a few months, Anne and Frederick were deeply in love, and they were engaged.

But Sir Walter was cold about it, and Anne's dear friend Lady Russell was worried. Wentworth had no money and no powerful friends, and a sailor's life was uncertain. Lady Russell loved Anne like a mother. Gently, again and again, she persuaded Anne to give him up. Anne believed it would be best for him, too.

Wentworth left, angry and hurt. Two years later, in 1808, he came back to England. Now he was a captain, with his own ship, the Laconia, and a few thousand pounds.

In the book, he's too proud to write to her. But this time, he picked up a pen.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LETTER]

Chapter two. The Letter.

The letter came to Kellynch Hall on a wet spring morning.

Sir Walter was busy admiring himself in one of his many looking-glasses. So nobody noticed when Anne carried the letter upstairs. She sat by her window and broke the seal with shaking fingers.

The letter was short. He said he had a ship now, and some money, and good hopes of more. He said he'd never stopped thinking of her. Then he asked one plain question. Would she have him now?

Anne read it three times. For two long years she'd been sure she had made the wrong choice.

She didn't need to think about her answer. She only needed to think about Lady Russell.

So she put on her bonnet and walked down the lane to Kellynch Lodge.

[Chapter card flips in: KELLYNCH LODGE]

Chapter three. Kellynch Lodge.

Lady Russell read the letter slowly. Then she looked at Anne for a long time.

For two years, she'd watched Anne grow quiet and pale.

"He was right about himself, wasn't he?" Lady Russell said at last. "He said he'd do well, and he has."

"Yes," said Anne softly.

Lady Russell had only ever wanted Anne to be safe and happy. Wentworth had a ship now, and money, and a good name in the navy. And Anne looked hopeful for the first time in two years.

"Then write to him, my dear," said Lady Russell. "And tell him yes."

Anne wrote back that same afternoon. Her letter was even shorter than his.

Ten days later, Captain Wentworth walked up the drive at Kellynch Hall. Anne was waiting at the door.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

They were married that autumn in the little church at Kellynch. Sir Walter was rather pleased, because the bridegroom was so tall and handsome.

Wentworth had always said he didn't like having women on board his ship. His sister, Mrs Croft, used to laugh at him for it. Once he had a wife, he changed his mind. When the Laconia sailed on a short trip along the coast, Anne went too.

She learned the names of the ropes and sails. On calm evenings, she and Wentworth stood together on the deck and watched the sun go down over the sea.

Anne still worried whenever there was news of a battle. But she was proud to be a sailor's wife. Six years sooner than in the book, Anne Elliot had her happy ending.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**Persuasion.** Anne followed advice that turned out to be wrong, and the novel asks when we should listen to others.

**Second chances.** Love can last through years apart, and it's never too late to try again.

**Rank and the navy.** Self-made sailors like Wentworth earn respect, while Sir Walter only has an old title.

**Being overlooked.** Anne's family ignores her, but she's often the wisest person in the room.

So what could have happened?

[Path card: A · She says no again]

Path A. Anne turns him down again, on Lady Russell's advice. It could happen: She was persuaded once before. But she's regretted that choice ever since.

[Path card: B · Sir Walter forbids it]

Path B. Sir Walter forbids the marriage. It could happen: He thinks the navy raises low-born men too high. But even in 1806, he never actually said no.

[Path card: C · "Would I!"]

Path C. Anne says yes, and Lady Russell gives her blessing. This one fits the characters: Anne has never stopped loving him. And Wentworth now has the rank and money Lady Russell worried about.

## The real ending

[clearly, storytelling]

So what actually happens in the book?

In the book, Wentworth is too proud to write, and they don't meet again until 1814. At Lyme, Louisa Musgrove is badly hurt in a fall on the Cobb, and later she becomes engaged to Captain Benwick. In Bath, Wentworth overhears Anne say that women love longest, and he writes her a letter, and at last they marry.

[thoughtfully]

Why did Jane Austen choose it? Austen shows that both of them had to grow, Anne in confidence and Wentworth in humility, before their second chance could work.

[Beat.]

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

Jane Austen wrote *Persuasion* at Chawton, in Hampshire, in 1815 and 1816, as her health was failing. She rewrote the ending, and the cancelled chapters are the only part of her published novels that survives in her own handwriting. She died in Winchester in July 1817, aged forty-one. *Persuasion* came out that December with *Northanger Abbey*, and a note by her brother Henry told readers her name for the first time.

## The world of the book

[curious]

*Persuasion* is set in 1814, when Britain thought its long war with Napoleon was over and naval officers were coming home. Many had grown rich from prize money, earned by capturing enemy ships. Jane Austen's own brothers, Francis and Charles, were officers in the Royal Navy.
