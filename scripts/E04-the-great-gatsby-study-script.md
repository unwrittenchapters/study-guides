## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1925 · Long Island and New York City, summer 1922]

[upbeat, storytelling]

Nick Carraway moves to West Egg, on Long Island, and rents a small house next to the mansion of the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Across the bay lives Nick's cousin Daisy and her husband, Tom Buchanan.

Gatsby throws giant parties every weekend. Nick learns that Gatsby and Daisy loved each other five years earlier, before the war, and that everything Gatsby owns is meant to win her back. Nick arranges for them to meet again over tea.

Gatsby wants to repeat the past exactly. The summer ends in a car accident, a death, and a funeral almost nobody attends. Nick goes home to the Midwest, thinking about the green light at the end of Daisy's dock.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: THE GREEN LIGHT]

Chapter one. The Green Light.

The story is told by a young man called Nick Carraway. He's come east from the Middle West to sell bonds in New York. He's rented a small, cheap house on Long Island, in a place called West Egg.

The book begins with some advice his father once gave him.

He means be slow to judge people, because you don't know what their lives have been like. Nick tries to live by that. It makes him a good listener, and that's why people tell him things.

Nick's little house sits right beside an enormous one. It's a huge, glittering mansion with a tower, a marble swimming pool, and more than forty acres of lawn and garden. It belongs to a mysterious man named Gatsby, and nobody seems to know anything about him.

[Chapter card flips in: TEA IN THE RAIN]

Chapter two. Tea in the Rain.

The next morning, there was a knock at Nick's door.

It was Gatsby, tanned and elegant in a beautiful pale suit, holding his hat in both hands like a schoolboy. He'd never been to Nick's house before. Nick thought he looked very nervous.

He said he had a favour to ask. Nick's cousin, Mrs Buchanan, was an old friend of his from a long time ago, and he'd very much like to see her again. He didn't want it to be at a party or at the big house. He wanted it to be quiet, just tea at Nick's little cottage, if Nick would be kind enough to invite her.

Nick said yes.

On the day of the tea, it rained. In fact it poured, a heavy, warm summer rain that came down all morning and drummed on the roof.

[Chapter card flips in: THE PAST]

Chapter three. The Past.

That summer, there were no parties at Gatsby's house.

The orchestras never came, and the lawn stayed quiet. On Saturday nights the great house stood dark, with only one or two windows lit. People drove out from the city hoping for champagne, found the gates shut, and drove away again, disappointed.

Instead, on warm afternoons, a small car would come across the bay from East Egg, and Daisy would get out. She and Gatsby and Nick would sit on the terrace, or walk down to the water, or take Gatsby's little boat out on the bay. They talked about Louisville, where Daisy grew up. They talked about the summer they'd fallen in love, five years before, when Gatsby was a young officer with no money and one uniform.

Gatsby was very happy that summer. But Nick noticed something.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

Gatsby closed up the great house at the end of the summer.

He told Nick he was going west to start again. He wasn't going to get rich, because he already was. He just wanted to find out who he was when he wasn't waiting for anyone. He said goodbye to Daisy on the terrace in the late September sunshine. He did it very kindly, the way you say goodbye to someone you'll always be glad you knew. She cried a little, and so did he.

Then, early one misty morning, his car went down the drive, past Nick's little house, and out onto the road, and he was gone.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**The past.** Gatsby believes you can repeat the past. The book suggests you can't.

**The American Dream.** Gatsby rebuilt himself from nothing. Fitzgerald asks whether money can buy what he really wants.

**Appearances.** The parties, the shirts and the mansion are all part of a show.

**Letting go.** Our version imagines Gatsby choosing to stop reaching for the green light.

## Our what-if

[excited]

So what's the what-if? Here's the turning point we changed.

In our story, Gatsby asks Nick for the tea in June, in person, with no parties first. The meeting goes the same way, awkward then joyful, but the summer stays quiet.

Everything after that moment is new, but the characters still act the way their authors wrote them.

[Beat.]

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

F. Scott Fitzgerald was born in Minnesota in 1896. He went to Princeton, served in the army during the First World War, and became famous young with his first novel. He and his wife Zelda became symbols of the Jazz Age. *The Great Gatsby* sold only modestly while he was alive, and it became a classic after his death in 1940.

## The world of the book

[curious]

The story takes place in 1922, during Prohibition, when alcohol was banned in the United States. Some people got rich very fast, and huge parties were a way to show off new money. Long Island's "Gold Coast" was full of mansions, and Fitzgerald himself lived nearby while he was planning the book.
