## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1606 · Scotland, the eleventh century]

[upbeat, storytelling]

After winning a great battle for King Duncan, the Scottish general Macbeth meets three witches on a heath. They greet him as Thane of Cawdor, and as the man who will be king. Moments later, messengers arrive to say the king has made him Thane of Cawdor.

Macbeth writes to his wife, and Lady Macbeth is sure the rest must come true. When King Duncan comes to stay at their castle, she urges her husband to kill him in his sleep.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: THE HEATH]

Chapter one. The Heath.

The battle was over, and Scotland had won.

Two of King Duncan's generals, Macbeth and Banquo, were riding home across a wild heath. Thunder rumbled overhead. Suddenly three strange figures stood in their path. They looked like women, but they had beards.

"All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Glamis!" said the first. That was already Macbeth's title.

"All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Cawdor!" said the second.

"All hail, Macbeth! that shalt be king hereafter!" said the third.

Then they vanished, like bubbles on water. Moments later, the king's messengers rode up. Duncan had made Macbeth the new Thane of Cawdor.

Macbeth wrote to his wife at once. When he got home, she was waiting for him. The king was coming to stay that very night, she said, and he must never leave. Macbeth understood exactly what she meant.

[Chapter card flips in: NO FURTHER]

Chapter two. No Further.

During the feast, Macbeth slipped out of the hall to think.

He paced up and down in the torchlight. Duncan was his cousin and his guest, and he'd been a good, gentle king. Only that day, he'd given Macbeth new honours.

Lady Macbeth came to find him. "Why have you left the chamber?" she asked.

Macbeth took a deep breath. "We will proceed no further in this business," he said.

She was furious. She called him a coward, and she said he didn't really love her. But tonight, Macbeth stood his ground. "I dare do all that may become a man," he told her. "Who dares do more is none."

He'd won the respect of everyone in Scotland, he said, and he wanted to enjoy it, not throw it away.

Lady Macbeth stared at him for a long time. Then she turned and went to bed without a word. That night, all through the castle, everyone slept.

[Chapter card flips in: THE MORNING]

Chapter three. The Morning.

Early the next morning, someone knocked loudly at the castle gate.

It was Macduff, the Thane of Fife. The king had asked him to call early. Macbeth walked him to Duncan's room himself, and waited outside the door with his heart pounding.

Then the door opened, and out came King Duncan, yawning and smiling. He'd slept well, he said, and the air at Inverness was the sweetest in Scotland.

Before he left, the king gave Lady Macbeth a diamond to thank her for being such a kind hostess. She held it in her hand and said nothing for a long while.

As the royal party rode away, Banquo fell in beside Macbeth. He said he'd dreamed about the three witches. Macbeth smiled and said he'd been thinking about them too.

"If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me without my stir," he said. Banquo laughed, and they rode on together.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

Years went by, and the witches' last promise never came true for Macbeth. He found that he didn't mind at all.

He fought for Scotland whenever Scotland needed him, and he came home to Inverness when the fighting was done. When Duncan's son Malcolm became king, Macbeth was one of his most trusted lords.

Lady Macbeth changed too. She planted a garden inside the castle walls and grew roses there. She kept the king's diamond in a little box by her bed. And she slept soundly every night, with clean hands and a quiet heart.

On summer evenings, Banquo rode over with his son, Fleance. The two old soldiers sat up on the castle walls and told stories about the battle where it all began. And they laughed about the strange sisters on the heath.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**Ambition.** Macbeth's hunger for power drives him to murder.

**Fate and free will.** The witches predict the future, but Macbeth chooses how to act.

**Guilt.** Blood and lost sleep haunt both Macbeths after the murder.

**Appearance and reality.** Things that look fair can be foul, and people hide what they really intend.

## Our what-if

[curious]

In an alternative scenario, what if Macbeth sticks to his decision and refuses to kill King Duncan?

[thoughtful]

So what could have happened?

[Path card: A · She does it herself]

Path A. Lady Macbeth kills the king herself. It could happen: She's fierce, and she's already made the plan. But in the play she admits she couldn't do it, because Duncan looked like her father as he slept.

[Path card: B · He gives in later]

Path B. Macbeth changes his mind again later that night. It could happen: He badly wants the crown, and his wife is very persuasive. But he's just listed every reason the murder is wrong.

[Path card: C · A loyal thane]

Path C. Macbeth stays loyal and leaves the crown to chance. This one fits the characters: Macbeth has already said that chance may crown him without his stir. And he knows that murder comes back to haunt the murderer.

[Verdict card]

The verdict: Path C. Shakespeare's Macbeth knows exactly why killing Duncan is wrong. Without his wife's push, his conscience would win.

## The real ending

[clearly, storytelling]

So what actually happens in the book?

In the play, Lady Macbeth persuades him, and Macbeth kills Duncan in his sleep and becomes king. To keep the crown, he has Banquo murdered and Macduff's family killed. Lady Macbeth, haunted by guilt, walks in her sleep and dies, and Macduff kills Macbeth in battle, so Malcolm becomes king.

[thoughtfully]

Why did William Shakespeare choose it? Shakespeare shows how one evil choice leads to another, until ambition destroys the person who has it.

[Beat.]

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

William Shakespeare was born in Stratford-upon-Avon in 1564 and became an actor and playwright in London. He probably wrote *Macbeth* around 1606, soon after King James of Scotland also became king of England. James was fascinated by witches and was said to be descended from Banquo, so the Scottish story suited him well. *Macbeth* was first printed in 1623, seven years after Shakespeare died.

## The world of the book

[curious]

The real Macbeth ruled Scotland for seventeen years, from 1040 to 1057, and he even made a pilgrimage to Rome. Shakespeare took the story from a history book, Holinshed's *Chronicles*, and changed many details. In his time many people believed in witches, and King James had written a book about them called *Daemonologie*.
