## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1819 · The Catskill Mountains, New York, before and after the American Revolution]

[upbeat, storytelling]

Rip Van Winkle is a kind, lazy man in a Dutch village at the foot of the Catskill Mountains. He'll help anyone with their work but never does his own, and his wife scolds him constantly.

One autumn day he walks up into the mountains with his dog, Wolf. A strange little man asks for help carrying a keg, and leads him to a hollow where men in old Dutch clothes are silently playing ninepins. Rip drinks from their flagon and falls asleep.

He wakes twenty years later. His gun has rusted, his dog is gone, and his beard is a foot long. In the village, King George's picture on the inn sign has become General Washington. The American Revolution has come and gone while he slept.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: THE KAATSKILL MOUNTAINS]

Chapter one. The Kaatskill Mountains.

Irving begins his story like this.

Kaatskill is the old Dutch spelling of Catskill. These mountains rise on the west side of the Hudson River in New York. Irving says they change colour with the weather and the time of day. The good wives of the villages used them like a barometer, which is a tool for telling the weather. When the mountains were clear and blue, it would be fair. When grey clouds gathered on top, a storm was coming.

At the foot of those mountains was a little village of old houses. The first Dutch settlers had built them with small yellow bricks and latticed windows. And in that village lived a man named Rip Van Winkle.

[Chapter card flips in: THUNDER IN THE HILLS]

Chapter two. Thunder in the Hills.

Rip looked around, but he saw nothing except a crow flying across the mountain. He thought he must have imagined it. Then he heard the same cry again, ringing through the still evening air. "Rip Van Winkle! Rip Van Winkle!"

Wolf bristled up his back, gave a low growl, and crept close to his master's side.

Then Rip saw a strange figure slowly climbing the rocks towards him, bent under something he carried on his back. It was a short, square old man with thick bushy hair and a grizzled beard. His clothes had gone out of fashion a hundred years before. He wore a cloth jacket and wide breeches with buttons down the sides, and on his shoulder he carried a keg.

[Chapter card flips in: MORNING]

Chapter three. Morning.

When Rip opened his eyes, it was morning.

He was lying on the green knoll at the top of the cliff, where he'd first seen the old man with the keg. The sun was just coming up over the mountains, and birds were hopping and twittering in the bushes. An eagle wheeled high overhead, catching the first of the light. Wolf sat beside him, wagging his tail and licking his face.

Rip sat up slowly and rubbed his eyes.

His gun lay beside him on the grass, as clean and bright as when he'd set it down. His beard was the same short, scrubby beard it had always been. The glen below was quiet and empty. There were no little men and no ninepins, no keg and no thunder. There was only a stream running down among the rocks, and the mist lifting slowly off the valley.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

Dame Van Winkle was standing in the doorway when he came up the lane. She had her hands on her hips and her mouth already open. She was ready to tell him what she thought about husbands who stayed out all night in the mountains.

And Rip did something he'd never done before. He walked straight up to her, kissed her on the cheek, and said he was sorry. Before she could get over her surprise, he went round the back of the house, picked up the hammer, and began to mend the fence.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**Time and change.** The village changes from a British colony to part of the United States while Rip sleeps. Irving shows history happening to ordinary people.

**Identity.** When Rip says "I'm not myself," he means the world has changed so much he doesn't know where he fits.

**Responsibility.** Our version asks what Rip would do if he got a warning instead of a lost twenty years.

**How stories grow.** Irving pretends the tale was found in an old historian's papers. Our version shows a true story getting bigger each time it's told.

## Our what-if

[excited]

So what's the what-if? Here's the turning point we changed.

In our story, Rip takes only one small sip and sleeps for a single night. But he dreams the whole twenty years, and wakes up knowing what he'd lose.

Everything after that moment is new, but the characters still act the way their authors wrote them.

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

Washington Irving was born in New York City in 1783, the year the Revolution ended. He was named after George Washington. He became one of the first American writers to be famous in Europe, and he spent years living in England and Spain. Later he settled at Sunnyside, his cottage on the Hudson River.

## The world of the book

[curious]

Irving wrote the story while living in England, after his family's business failed. He set it in the Hudson Valley, where Dutch families had farmed since the 1600s. The story spans the years before and after the American Revolution, when the colonies became a new country.
