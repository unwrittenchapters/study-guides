## The book in brief

[Card slides in: 1850 · Boston, Massachusetts, 1640s]

[upbeat, storytelling]

In Puritan Boston in the 1640s, Hester Prynne stands on a wooden scaffold in the marketplace, holding her baby, Pearl. She's been found guilty of adultery, and she must wear a scarlet letter A on her dress for the rest of her life. She refuses to name the baby's father.

In the crowd is her long-missing husband. He takes a new name, Roger Chillingworth, and sets out to find the man. His search leads him to the town's beloved young minister, Arthur Dimmesdale, who is growing sick with a secret.

## The story, chapter by chapter

[storytelling, warm but lively]

Now for the story. Here's how it unfolds, chapter by chapter.

[Chapter card flips in: THE SCAFFOLD]

Chapter one. The Scaffold.

One summer morning in old Boston, a crowd waited outside the prison door.

A young woman stepped out into the sunshine, holding a baby. Her name was Hester Prynne. On her dress was a letter A, made of red cloth and stitched with gold thread. It showed that she'd had a child with a man who wasn't her husband.

She had to stand on the scaffold for three hours while the whole town stared. The ministers begged her to name the baby's father. The young Reverend Dimmesdale spoke most gently of all. But Hester shook her head. She would never tell.

Seven years passed. Hester lived in a little cottage by the sea and sewed for the town. Her daughter, Pearl, grew into a quick, clever, wild girl. And Mr. Dimmesdale grew thinner and paler every year, with his hand pressed over his heart.

Then, one dark night in May, Dimmesdale crept out of his house and climbed the scaffold by himself.

[Chapter card flips in: THE PROMISE]

Chapter two. The Promise.

He stood there in the dark, cold and ashamed.

Then he heard footsteps. Hester and Pearl were walking home from a deathbed, and he called them up to join him. The three of them stood together, holding hands. Warmth rushed through him like new life.

Pearl looked up at him. "Wilt thou stand here with mother and me, tomorrow noontide?" she asked.

Dimmesdale opened his mouth to say no. He'd been afraid for seven long years. But Pearl's small hand was warm in his, and he was so tired of hiding.

"Yes," he whispered. "Tomorrow, at noon."

Pearl didn't laugh or tease him. She just squeezed his hand tighter.

Just then a great light flashed across the sky. In its red glow, Dimmesdale saw old Roger Chillingworth watching them from the street below. For once, the minister didn't look away.

[Chapter card flips in: NOON]

Chapter three. Noon.

By noon the next day, the marketplace was busy with people buying and selling.

They were surprised to see Hester Prynne climb the scaffold steps with Pearl. They were even more surprised when Mr. Dimmesdale followed. He took Pearl's hand, and then Hester's, and turned to face the town.

His voice shook at first. Then it grew clear and strong, the way it did in church. He told them that he was Pearl's father. He'd let Hester carry the shame alone for seven years, he said, and he would hide no longer.

Nobody spoke. Old Mr. Wilson slowly took off his hat. Governor Bellingham frowned and looked down at his boots. Some people turned away. But many of them remembered the long nights Hester had sat with their sick children.

At the foot of the scaffold, Roger Chillingworth stood very still. There was no secret left for him to use.

[Chapter card flips in: THE LAST PAGE]

The Last Page.

Mr. Dimmesdale lost his place as minister. But for the first time in years, he slept through the night.

He began teaching the town's children to read, in a small schoolroom near the shore. Hester still sewed, and now and then she'd look up from her needle to watch him with the children. Pearl, who had once seemed half wild, was the best reader in the class, and she made sure everyone knew it.

Hester still wore the scarlet letter. But as the years went by, people began to say the A stood for Able, because she was so strong and so helpful.

On Sunday afternoons, the three of them walked along the beach together. Pearl swung between them, holding a hand on each side, in the bright, open daylight.

## The big ideas

[curious]

**Sin and guilt.** Hidden guilt hurts Dimmesdale far more than public shame hurts Hester.

**Public judgment.** The Puritan town punishes Hester in front of everyone.

**Hypocrisy.** The most admired man in town hides the very sin he preaches against.

**Identity.** Over time, Hester changes what her scarlet A means.

## Our what-if

[curious]

In an alternative scenario, what if Dimmesdale says yes when Pearl asks him to stand with them at noon?

[thoughtful]

So what could have happened?

[Path card: A · The town drives them out]

Path A. The angry town drives the whole family away. It could happen: Puritan Boston punished sin harshly and in public. But by now, Hester has earned respect by caring for the sick and the poor.

[Path card: B · He backs out]

Path B. He loses his nerve by morning and stays silent. It could happen: He's kept his secret for seven years. But he's made a promise to Pearl, and he's longed to confess for a long time.

[Path card: C · He tells the truth]

Path C. He stands on the scaffold at noon and tells the whole town the truth. This one fits the characters: Dimmesdale is sick from hiding his guilt. And Hester and Pearl are right there beside him.

## The real ending

[clearly, storytelling]

So what actually happens in the book?

In the book, Dimmesdale says no, and later he and Hester plan to escape to Europe by ship. Instead, after his greatest sermon, he climbs the scaffold with Hester and Pearl, confesses to the town, and dies in Hester's arms. Chillingworth dies within a year and leaves Pearl a fortune.

[thoughtfully]

Why did Nathaniel Hawthorne choose it? Hawthorne shows that hidden guilt destroys a person, and that telling the truth, even late, brings a kind of peace.

[Beat.]

## Meet the author

[upbeat]

Before we go, here's a little about who wrote it.

Nathaniel Hawthorne was born in Salem, Massachusetts, in 1804. One of his ancestors, John Hathorne, was a judge at the Salem witch trials, and Hawthorne wrote that he felt ashamed of it. He worked at the Salem Custom House until he lost the job in 1849. He wrote *The Scarlet Letter* soon after, and it came out in 1850.

## The world of the book

[curious]

The Puritans who founded Boston in 1630 wanted their colony to follow strict religious rules. Church and law were closely tied, and punishments were often public, so the whole town could watch. Hawthorne was writing about two hundred years later, looking back at his own ancestors' world.
